The AI boom’s power bill is turning into a health bill

Former E.P.A. officials warn that gas-fired power for data centers could add at least $20 billion in annual health costs by 2028.

The data-center buildout is usually discussed in terms of chips, electricity demand and cloud capacity. This warning reframes it in more human terms: air pollution from gas-fired power plants used to run those facilities. That figure is less a footnote than a challenge to the industry’s clean, abstract vocabulary around “AI infrastructure.”

A zero-click WeChat worm demo shows how AI changes exploit work

Calif Research says it built a cross-platform WeChat call worm after using AI to find and exploit a remote-code-execution bug in days.

Simon Willison flags a startling claim from Calif Research: a demo worm, dubbed WeWorm, that spreads through WeChat calls across iOS and Android without the victim answering or touching the phone. The quoted researchers say AI helped them find the bug and write the first remote code execution exploit in about two days, with the worm taking one more week. Their larger point is the unsettling one: work that once required a larger team over months may now be accelerated by AI, while humans provide target selection and safety judgment. This is framed as a demo, not a reported wild outbreak.

Why it matters: If the claim holds, AI is not merely helping defenders write reports — it is compressing the timeline for serious offensive security research.

Sources: Simon Willison
