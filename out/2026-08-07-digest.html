The reason is straightforward: Federal Reserve officials remain chiefly focused on the trajectory of inflation after five years of running above the central bank’s 2 percent target. In other words, a cooling labor market may complicate the policy picture, but it does not settle it.

Why it matters: Markets often treat weak jobs data as a rate-cut signal. This is a reminder that the Fed’s reaction function is still anchored in inflation credibility, not just employment softness.
