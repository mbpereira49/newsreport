The film is described as a drama about a Danish servant in the uneasy years after World War II — a premise that sounds intimate rather than spectacle-driven, and very much in the festival tradition of rewarding historical unease rendered through private life. Lee Chang-dong’s “Possible Love” won the runner-up award.

Why it matters: Festival prizes are not box-office prophecies, but they are powerful signals: they shape distribution attention, awards-season narratives, and which international films enter the wider conversation rather than staying on the festival circuit.

Soho’s restaurant map sits on top of an older city

Eater’s editor-picked guide folds today’s dining scene into the neighborhood’s industrial, cast-iron past.

Eater NY’s Soho restaurant guide starts with a useful reminder: before Soho became a shorthand for art, shopping, loft apartments, and destination dining, it was largely industrial and commercial, defined by Victorian-era cast-iron architecture. By the 1960s and ’70s, the neighborhood had become rundown enough for artists to move in and make studios in those distinctive buildings. The current list of “best restaurants,” then, lands in a place already shaped by cycles of abandonment, reinvention, and taste-making.

Why it matters: Restaurant guides are often treated as pure consumption tools. In Soho, the where-to-eat question is inseparable from the neighborhood’s long conversion of industrial space into cultural cachet — and then into expensive appetite.
