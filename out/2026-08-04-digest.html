Please don’t become a ‘meat proxy’ for AI

A useful new insult names a common failure mode: copying AI output to other humans without reading, checking, or owning it.

Niklas Gruhn’s phrase “meat proxy,” highlighted by Simon Willison, is funny because it is too precise. It describes people who blindly copy and paste AI-generated text to peers, outsourcing not just drafting but judgment. Willison’s prescription is simple and humane: prompt AI if you want, but read the output, understand it, validate it, and then respond in your own words. The value is not in moving text from machine to person. It is in adding discernment before you do.

Why it matters: As AI slips into everyday work, the scarce skill is not generating words. It is being accountable for them.
