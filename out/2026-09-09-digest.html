Why it matters: When yields rise despite an intervention meant to reduce borrowing costs, it is a clean vote of skepticism. The Terence Tao quote, posted by Simon Willison, is a sharp version of a growing anxiety: open science depends on researchers being willing to share unfinished ideas, but AI may change the incentives. Tao describes “good, fruitful open problems” as something being mined in a “non-renewable fashion.” More strikingly, he warns that even a rumor of someone working on a problem can trigger AI-powered efforts to “flatten it” before the original project matures.

Why it matters: This is not just a math-world etiquette dispute. If AI systems make it easier to swarm partially formed research leads, scholars may become more secretive. That would reverse a deep norm of open scientific exchange—not because openness stopped being virtuous, but because it became strategically dangerous.
