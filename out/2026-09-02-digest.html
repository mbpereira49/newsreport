Employer health costs are projected to jump sharply in 2027

A new survey points to an 11 percent average increase unless companies trim benefits.

Employer-sponsored health coverage may be headed for one of its steepest cost increases in decades. A new U.S. survey anticipates an average 11 percent rise in 2027 unless employers cut benefits, with other findings also pointing to a sharp increase. That sets up a familiar but painful choice: absorb higher costs, shift more of them to workers, or narrow coverage.

Why it matters: For many Americans, employer insurance is the health system. A spike this large would show up in paychecks, household budgets and benefit design—not just corporate expense lines.

Sources: NYT Business

Two deaths in LAPD custody put mental-health policing back under scrutiny

Graphic video prompted investigations and questions from Mayor Karen Bass about officer training.

The deaths of two men in Los Angeles police custody have triggered investigations after the city released graphic video of officers’ actions. Mayor Karen Bass questioned whether police are adequately trained to handle mental health crises, placing the cases squarely inside the city’s long-running debate over what officers are asked to do—and whether they are equipped to do it safely.

Why it matters: Custody deaths test public trust at the most basic level. When mental health is part of the call, the stakes extend beyond one department to the design of emergency response itself.

New York City’s richest residents—down to the top .001 percent—have grown even richer, according to a new report from the city comptroller. Claude’s system prompt now draws a bright line around song lyrics

Anthropic’s published consumer prompts show a conspicuous tightening around copyrighted lyric reproduction.

Simon Willison notes that Anthropic continues to publish system prompts for its Claude consumer apps—Claude.ai and the mobile apps, though not Claude Cowork or Claude Code—and now organizes them by model with historical versions. The detail that jumps out: Claude’s newer prompt appears especially emphatic about not reproducing song lyrics. Willison praises the transparency, including the availability of past prompt changes, while pointing out the documentation site is itself designed to be usable by LLMs.

Why it matters: System prompts are policy in executable form. When a major AI lab publicly shows how it steers models around copyrighted material, it offers a rare view into the practical, evolving boundary between usefulness and legal risk.

Sources: Simon Willison

The Saturday Evening Post is ending print after 205 years

The storied magazine will continue online, closing a long chapter of American print culture.

The Saturday Evening Post, known for publishing Jack London stories and Norman Rockwell art, will stop printing after 205 years. The publication will continue online, but the end of the physical magazine is still a symbolic marker: another legacy title severing its identity from the format that made it famous.

Why it matters: Print’s decline is often told in quarterly losses and ad shifts. This one lands differently because the Post was not just a magazine brand but a vessel for a particular image of American mass culture.

Sources: NYT Business

A rare bit of conservation joy: 325 kākāpō

After a record breeding season, juveniles have been added to the critically endangered parrot’s population count.

Andrew Digby reports that the kākāpō population has reached 325 after chicks from this year’s record breeding season became juveniles and were added to the count. In 1995, just 51 kākāpō remained. Simon Willison quotes the update as “the best news of the year,” and the numbers make the optimism feel earned rather than sentimental.

Why it matters: Recovery for critically endangered species is slow, fragile work. This is a reminder that sustained conservation effort can move the line from managed decline toward genuine rebound.

Sources: Simon Willison
