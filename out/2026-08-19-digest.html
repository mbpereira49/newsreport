The bond rout is becoming everyone’s problem

Rising yields are adding another cost shock at a moment when investors were already on edge.

The New York Times’ DealBook frames the latest rise in global bond yields as an escalation, not an isolated market wobble. Investors were already rattled by several factors; higher yields make the pressure more concrete by raising the price of money. That flows through to governments, companies, mortgages and risk assets. The headline phrase “global bond rout” suggests a broad selloff, while the excerpt’s practical point is simpler: when yields climb, financing gets more expensive for almost everyone.

Why it matters: Bond yields are the plumbing of the economy. A sustained move higher can tighten conditions without any new central-bank decision, squeezing borrowers and repricing stocks, housing and public debt all at once.

Mojo finally goes open source, with a quieter Python promise

The compiler and toolchain are now under Apache 2.0, but the language’s original “Python superset” ambition has softened.

Mojo, the programming language that has courted Python users and AI developers, has followed its 1.0 release by open-sourcing its compiler and toolchain under an Apache 2 license. Simon Willison notes that this fulfills a promise dating back to May 2023. The more interesting wrinkle is strategic: Mojo originally pitched itself as a superset of Python, a way to bootstrap adoption from existing Python code. That framing changed around August 2025; the excerpt says Mojo “may or may not” become a full Python superset, and that this is now treated as acceptable. The new bet appears to lean on AI-assisted coding tools to help migrate Python to Mojo instead of requiring perfect language compatibility.

Why it matters: Open sourcing removes a major adoption barrier, but the shift in ambition matters just as much. Mojo is asking developers to believe less in seamless Python continuity and more in tooling-assisted migration.

Sources: Simon Willison
